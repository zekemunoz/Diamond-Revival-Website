Why Coating

A wax lasts a month. A coating lasts years.

Here's what's actually happening to your paint — and what a ceramic coating does about it.

The problem: swirl marks

Automatic washes, dirty towels and one-bucket washing drag grit across your clear coat. Thousands of tiny scratches. In the sun they look like a spiderweb — and they're the reason a black car never looks black anymore.

The fix: paint correction

A machine polisher levels the clear coat and removes the scratches — permanently. Not filled, not hidden. Removed. This is the step that makes paint look better than the day it left the dealer.

The protection: ceramic

A liquid glass coating chemically bonds to the clear coat and cures into a hard, slick shell. Dirt doesn't stick. Water sheets off. Bird droppings and bug guts stop etching your paint. And you never wax again.

The Process

What actually happens to your car

A coating is not a product you buy. It's a process you buy. Here's every step, in order.

01

Free paint inspection

I come to you. I put a professional inspection light on your paint and show you exactly what's in the clear coat — most people have never seen it. Then I take paint-depth readings on every panel so we know how much clear coat there is to safely work with. You get a written finding and three options. No pressure, no obligation.

~20 minutes · Free
02

Decontamination

Foam pre-soak, two-bucket hand wash, iron remover to dissolve the brake dust embedded in the paint, then a clay bar to pull out everything the wash left behind. Skip this and you polish the contamination into the paint.

Day 1, morning
03

Paint correction

The long part. Machine polishing, panel by panel, with depth readings taken as I go so I never take more clear coat than is safe. I'll pull the car back into the light and show you 50/50 — half corrected, half untouched — before I go any further.

Day 1–2 · the bulk of the job
04

Coating application

Panels wiped down with a prep solvent to strip every trace of polish oil, then the coating is applied by hand, panel by panel, and leveled at exactly the right moment. Too early and it smears. Too late and it high-spots. This is where experience shows.

Final evening
05

Cure & handoff

The coating needs to sit dry and untouched in your garage overnight. No rain, no dust, no driving. Next morning I walk you around it, hand you the aftercare kit, and show you how to wash it so the coating lasts as long as it's supposed to.

12–24 hours to cure · no water for 7 days
Before You Book

What I need from you

Coatings are chemistry, and chemistry has conditions. Read this before booking — it protects your money and my work.

Requirements

  • A garage. The coating must cure dust-free and dry overnight. This is the one thing I can't work around — no garage, no coating. If you don't have one, I'll point you to the Diamond Revival Full Detail instead.
  • 1 to 3 days of the car sitting still. Correction can't be rushed. If you need the car back same-day, this isn't the service for you.
  • Power and water access. Standard outlet and a spigot. I bring everything else — water tank, generator, lighting, all product.
  • A clean-out. Personal items removed from the interior before I arrive.

Straight talk

  • A coating is not a force field. It won't stop rock chips, door dings, or a shopping cart. It stops chemical and environmental damage — etching, staining, oxidation, bonded contamination.
  • If the clear coat is already failing — peeling, chalky, or previously sanded through — no coating will save it. I'll tell you that at the inspection and I won't take your money.
  • The warranty depends on you washing it right. Automatic brush washes will ruin a coating faster than anything on earth. I'll show you the two-bucket method and send you home with the kit.
  • Fully insured, including care, custody & control on your vehicle while it's in my hands.
Questions

The stuff people actually ask

Why does this cost so much more than a detail?

Because it's a different job. A full detail is 4 hours of cleaning. A coating is 16–30 hours of machine work on your clear coat plus several hundred dollars of professional product that a consumer can't buy off a shelf. Look at it per year instead of per job: the 5-year Armor on a sedan is $1,199, which is about $20 a month for five years of never waxing, never scrubbing bugs off, and a car that still looks new when you sell it.

Can't I just buy a ceramic spray at the parts store?

Those are sealants with ceramic in the name. They sit on top of the paint and last weeks to a couple of months. A professional coating is a different chemistry — it bonds to the clear coat and cures into a hard layer measured in years. And more importantly, a spray goes on top of your swirl marks. It locks the damage in. Correction is the part that actually changes how the car looks.

Do I really need the correction, or can I just get the coating?

You can coat over damaged paint. I just won't do it without telling you what you're getting: every scratch that's in the clear coat right now gets sealed under a hard layer for the next five years. If the paint is genuinely clean — a new car, garage kept — Shield's light polish is enough. If it's been through automatic washes, you want correction. The inspection tells us which.

Is it safe? Can polishing damage my paint?

In the wrong hands, yes — that's exactly why I measure. Clear coat is only about 1.5 to 2 mils thick, and I take depth readings on every panel before and during correction so I always know how much I'm working with. If a panel reads thin, I back off and tell you. Anyone who polishes a car without a paint gauge is guessing with your money.

What if I have a brand new car?

Best possible time. New paint still has swirls in it from dealer prep — but very few — so a light polish gets it near-perfect, and then the coating goes on before the damage ever starts. A new car is the cheapest car to protect and the one that benefits most.

How do I wash it after?

Two buckets, a pH-neutral soap, and a clean microfiber. That's it. Never an automatic brush wash. The coating makes washing dramatically easier — dirt barely holds on — but it's not immune to being scratched by a dirty rag. I'll walk you through it at handoff and send you home with the products to do it.

How do I pay, and is there a deposit?

A $200 deposit holds your date and comes off the total. Balance is due at handoff. Card, Zelle, or cash — whatever's easiest for you.
